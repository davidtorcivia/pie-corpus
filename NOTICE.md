**Lexicon and paradigm data** (`data/lexicon/`, `data/morph/inflection_specs.jsonl`, and lexicon fields copied into `corpus/`): derived from **Wiktionary** via the kaikki.org wiktextract dump of the English Wiktionary (2026-10-01). Wiktionary content is licensed under the [Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). These derived files are distributed under the same license, with attribution to the Wiktionary contributors. Source details: `raw/MANIFEST.md`.

**Tier 1 scholarly texts** (`raw/tier1-*/versions.json`, `corpus/tier1/`): short reconstructed texts (Schleicher's fable, *The King and the God*) quoted for research, each with its full citation. The reconstructions remain the work of their authors: August Schleicher (1868, public domain), Hermann Hirt, Winfred P. Lehmann & Ladislav Zgusta, Danka, Douglas Q. Adams, Rosemarie Lühr, Joseph Voyles & Charles Barrack, Frederik Kortlandt, Andrew Miles Byrd, Eric Hamp, Subhadra Kumar Sen. Wikipedia transcriptions are CC BY-SA 4.0. The provenance of each line is recorded in its record.

**Synthetic data** (`corpus/tier3/`): generated by this project's engine from plans written by language models. The provenance is in each record's `generator` field. Synthetic PIE is plausible by construction, not recovered.

**Reference-book facts** (`data/lexicon/liv2.jsonl`, `data/themes/themes.jsonl`, the `liv` fields in `data/lexicon/lexicon.jsonl`, and the book corrections in `data/morph/closed_class.json`): factual data (root reconstructions, short glosses, stem formations, page and IEW references, motif labels) taken from H. Rix et al., *Lexikon der indogermanischen Verben*, 2nd ed. (2001); M. L. West, *Indo-European Poetry and Myth* (2007); B. W. Fortson IV, *Indo-European Language and Culture*, 2nd ed. (2010); D. Ringe, *From Proto-Indo-European to Proto-Germanic*, 2nd ed. (2017). Each record cites its page or section. No running text from the books is included; theme descriptions are paraphrases. The books themselves are not distributed.
