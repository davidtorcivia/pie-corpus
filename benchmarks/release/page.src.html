Release · PIE Nemotron 120B

A model that speaks Proto‑Indo‑European.

PIE Nemotron 120B is NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super fine-tuned on a reconstructed corpus of the language. On our pre-registered benchmark it scores 88.5, ahead of every frontier model we tested at every reasoning setting. In the project's exact notation it scores 86.2, where the best frontier run reaches 56.5.

The lead comes from the grammar

Model

How it was built

Base
NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super 120B-A12B hybrid Mamba–attention mixture of experts, 12B active
Method
LoRA rank 16, Tinker's Dimuon optimizer, lr 8e-3 chosen by a six-probe sweep
Data
42,932 conversations, 10.8M tokens per epoch, 3 epochs 2,514 steps of 64
Compute
About $41 of training on Tinker
Serving
5-bit, 79 GB, on one Mac thinking off; greedy decoding for the benchmark

The corpus is reconstructed Proto-Indo-European written to one consistent standard: inflection drills from the project's morphology engine, paradigms, translations both ways, and dialogue set in the speakers' own world. Its forms are checked against the engine.

The checkpoint with the best held-out loss was epoch 1, yet epoch 3 won the benchmark. We now judge every epoch on the benchmark rather than trusting the loss.

Reinforcement learning on top of epoch 3 is under way, rewarded by the engine and a Claude judge.

Method

The benchmark

Pre-registered on 1 November 2026, before any contestant ran. 607 items, none of them in any training data.

Inflection · 20
335 items. One form, exact match to the engine.
Paradigms · 15
124 items. Share of the table's cells given right.
EN → PIE · 30
42 items. Judged: does the PIE say what the English says?
PIE → EN · 20
42 items. Judged: does the English say what the PIE says?
Dialogue · 15
64 items. Judged: an in-world, grammatical PIE reply.

Two judges from two vendors, so no vendor grades its own models alone: Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol, both at high effort, both with the project's engine and corpus tools and the reference answer. A segment's score is the mean of the two.

Lenient forgives notation only: accents, vowel length, ASCII spellings and case. A wrong ending is still wrong. Strict also requires every word to be a real form in the project's notation.

Frontier models answered from their weights alone: no tools, no web, no project files. Weights by segment favor English → PIE, the main use of the translation app.

The benchmark is this project's own, and our models were trained on its notation and corpus (the test items were held out). The strict score therefore favors them by design; the lenient score, which forgives notation, is the headline.

PIE Nemotron 120B e3 and e1 were scored unquantized (bf16). Epoch 1 at 5 bits scored 87.6 against 87.1 unquantized. The Qwen models and Nemotron epoch 2 were scored at 5 bits.
