# Knee-supported sleep: results of the pre-registered tests (2026-10-28)

Design: [knee-sleep-preregistration.md](knee-sleep-preregistration.md) (commit 370c0ac, before any test ran).
Detailed reports: [Test A, Near East](knee-sleep/testA-neareast.md), [Test A, East Asia, Finnic and Arabic](knee-sleep/testA-east.md),
[Tests B and C](knee-sleep/testB-testC.md). Owner's packet: [knee-sleep/packet/](knee-sleep/packet/).

## Summary

Under the pre-registered rules, the proposed inherited PIE association KNEE[SUPPORT]–SLEEP/DREAM is not
supported as phraseology. Unrelated traditions produce the scene (Test A). The \*swep- sleep words are not a
marked choice in knee scenes (Tests B, C). The Hittite–Greek match is explained by an ordinary human scene
told with each language's ordinary "be asleep" word. What survives is narrower: knee-supported sleep is a
widespread scene, and it is attested in Hittite and Greek.

## Test A: typological control. Result: the scene is ordinary

| Tradition | Explicit positives (new) | Locus |
|---|---|---|
| Sumerian | 1 | Gilgamesh and Huwawa A (ETCSL 1.8.1.5) l. 91: "as if sleeping on the knee (*du₁₀*) of my mother Ninsumun" (simile) |
| Japanese | 3 scenes | Kojiki/Nihon shoki, Suinin (712/720): the emperor sleeps on the empress's knee; Nihon shoki 14, Ankō: sleeps on the empress's knee and is killed asleep; Genji, Momiji no ga (c. 1008) |
| Chinese | 1 | Taiping guangji 38, Li Mi (Tang story): asks to sleep once on the emperor's knee |
| Hebrew (registered beforehand, recall check) | – | Judg 16:19; 2 Kgs 4:20 |
| Akkadian, Egyptian, Finnic, Arabic, Mongolian | 0 | thin open corpora for Akkadian narrative and Egyptian |

The rule's wording was ambiguous: should the two registered Hebrew items count for their tradition? Both
readings were recorded. With new finds in three unrelated traditions (Sumerian, Japanese, Chinese), the rule
is met either way. The Japanese chronicles are written in literary Chinese, but Man'yōshū 14.3457 (*piza maku*,
spelled phonetically) and the vernacular Genji show the scene is native Japanese as well.

Contrasts: most traditions prefer another support for sleep or death: the bosom in Hebrew (6, including
1 Kgs 17:19, Elijah's twin of 2 Kgs 4:20), the lap or arms in Sumerian (7), the arm-pillow in Japanese (19 against
1 knee-pillow poem), the thigh in Chinese and Arabic. A recurring plot appears across unrelated traditions: a man
asleep on the knees of a woman who plots against him (Judg 16:19; Suinin; Ankō).

## Test B: Hittite *šupp-* vs *šeš-*. Result: no knee preference

TLHdig, 92,183 paragraphs; 16 genuine *šupp-* tokens after reading. Recall: KUB 36.89 rev. 56′–59′ found.

| Feature | *šupp-* with / without | *šeš-* with / without | one-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|
| knee (*genu-*) | 1 / 12 | 1 / 215 | 0.11 (descriptive) |
| knee or dream (registered rule) | 4 / 9 | 6 / 210 | 0.0011 |

The knee-or-dream rule is formally met, but not because of knees. Three of the four *šupp-* hits are *tešḫa-*
used for "sleep" ("sleep seized him", "does not sleep a sleep", and KUB 36.89 itself). *zašḫai-* "dream"
never occurs with either verb. The two paragraphs that have both verbs show why: *šeš-* is "lie down / lie
with" (KUB 20.86+: "sleeps with his wife (*šešzi*) or sleeps (*šuppariyattari*)"), and *šupp-* is "be asleep".
A description of a sleeper on knees would pick *šupp-* for that reason alone. The famous incubation phrase uses
*šeš-* (*šuppa šeškiškanzi*, "sleep in purity").

## Test C: Greek ὕπνος/ὑπνόω vs other sleep words. Result: no preference

The \*swep- share of all sleep units is 47% in Homer, 51% in archaic–classical, and 54% in later Greek.

- The rule as registered fails here: verse units are single lines, so knee and sleep words never share a
  unit, and the recall check fails at unit level.
- In a documented ±2-line window, two positives were read: AP 7.170 (Posidippus 7.170; κοιμαθείς + ὕπνον)
  and Hymn to Demeter 263–264 (ἰαύω).
- Their \*swep- share is at or below base rate (p 0.78–0.90). Neither sleep *verb* is \*swep-.

## Corrections and method notes

- KUB 36.89's knee word is syllabic *ge-nu-wa-〈aš〉* in TLHdig, with an editorial ending. The packet's "GI-NU-WA"
  should be checked against the handcopy. TLHdig has no logographic knee at all.
- Rule weaknesses, recorded rather than repaired after the fact:
  - Test A's counting of the registered items was ambiguous.
  - Test C's within-unit rule cannot work for verse.
  - "Tiny counts" was not given a number.
- Corpus finds:
  - Greek lemma indexes drop elided forms (γούνατ', εὕδοντ'); matching by spelling recovers them.
  - TLHdig needs its ambiguity prefixes stripped and non-Hittite words excluded.
  - Kanripo spells "knee" 䣛 or 厀 in places.

## What this means for the earlier work

- **The owner's packet.** Its source control is careful, and its narrower statements stand: the scenes
  exist, and the transmission relations it traces are real. Its central proposal is weakened. The open
  question it posed, inheritance or local combination, now leans clearly toward local combination of a common
  scene with ordinary vocabulary.
- **The Stage C-S knee-sleep lead** (Norse ~ Hittite) is downgraded on the same grounds (`data/research/8c/leads.jsonl`).
- **One avenue remains: Mesopotamian contact.** The Sumerian Gilgamesh positive and the Hittite Gilgamesh
  translations make a contact route to the Hittite passage possible. That question is about areal history, not
  PIE, and it is open.
