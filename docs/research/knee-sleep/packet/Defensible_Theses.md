# The defensible theses
## Claims, warrants and limits

**28 October 2026 · consolidated through research round 36**

This brief states the strongest version of the argument supported by the available record. “Secure” below describes a bounded textual observation, not a claim of deductive certainty about prehistory. The proposed historical inference remains defeasible. The full paper, evidence catalogue and source/read-scope register accompany this brief.

## Thesis 1 — The core comparison is substantive, not a pair of isolated etymologies

**Claim.** The Hittite Nerik passage and AP7.170 align inherited knee vocabulary, *swep-family sleep material and the role of a sleeper supported on knees. This is a positive lexical–semantic correspondence.

**Warrant.** KUB36.89 rev56′–59′ has GI-NU-WA and šuppariyanza; the Greek closing clause has ἐπὶ γούνων ματρός and ὕπνον. In each, the same participant is the sleeper or the person represented as asleep and the supported participant. The relation therefore belongs to the wording of the scenes, not merely to their modern English translations. [S001–S002,S006;E01–E02]

**Limit.** A real correspondence does not by itself identify the date at which the pairing became conventional. It warrants an inheritance proposal; it does not prove that convention.

## Thesis 2 — The proposed reconstruction is a flexible association, not an invented sentence

**Claim.** The defensible object of reconstruction is KNEE[SUPPORT]–SLEEP/DREAM. No complete inflected PIE sentence is recovered.

**Warrant.** The participant relation persists despite different grammar: a Hittite sleep derivative with ēšta and dream content versus a Greek sleep participle plus HAVE governing a sleep noun. A reconstructed HEAD–PUT clause would add material absent from the Greek anchor and unnecessary to the defined relation. [E01–E02]

**Limit.** Calling the unit flexible cannot license any nearby mention of knees and sleep. The sleeper must be the supported participant. Prayer, anatomical injury and temporally separate actions remain different constructions.

## Thesis 3 — The Greek funerary meaning qualifies, but does not erase, the positive

**Claim.** AP7.170 is a knee-supported DEATH-AS-SLEEP attestation.

**Warrant.** The mother recovers the child from the well, and the final words place him on her knees in deep sleep. The funerary setting is explicit in the literary context and editorial classification. The text still uses inherited sleep vocabulary in the target relation. [S006;E02]

**Limit.** It is not unqualified evidence for an ordinary nap or dream. AP7.173 shows a funerary sleep metaphor without knees, so “deep/long sleep” alone is not a distinctive knee-associated diagnostic. [S007;E03]

## Thesis 4 — The Hittite archaism is a conditional contribution

**Claim.** Absence of -za can support older usage locally under the second-person predicative-BE analysis of ēšta.

**Warrant.** The grammatical analysis noted in the research record and dictionary is specific to that reading. It offers an additional reason to investigate whether older phrasing is embedded in the extant ritual. [S001;E05]

**Limit.** Third-person/SIT analyses do not produce the same inference. Participial -nza is not the reflexive clitic. An apparent archaism is not a date for the whole composite work and is not automatically a feature shared with the Greek passage. [S020]

## Thesis 5 — Support by one’s own knees is relevant evidence

**Claim.** Self-supported knee sleep or dreaming falls within the target relation.

**Warrant.** The relationship under investigation is bodily support and sleep, not a mandatory two-person interaction. The Persian episodes illustrate the same relation with the supporter and sleeper sharing an identity. [S047;E19–E20]

**Limit.** Self-support cannot remove a textual negation or convert subsequent dreaming into an explicitly affirmative sleep statement. Nicholson’s bi-xuft / na-xuft variant must remain visible.

## Thesis 6 — Later attestation may contribute without being backdated

**Claim.** Persian, Irish, Norse, Latin and Scottish material contributes evidence of semantic range, continuity or transformation.

**Warrant.** The record contains actual knee-supported sleep scenes and, in several cases, direct manuscript observations. Such material is not automatically irrelevant because it is later than the Hittite–Greek anchors. [E19–E31,E34]

**Limit.** A late edition is not a medieval witness; a manuscript date is not necessarily the composition date of its attached prose; a narrated event year is not the date of the words. The historical weight must be assigned separately to each layer.

## Thesis 7 — Copies and translations are evidence of transmission, not extra branches

**Claim.** The Irish Óengus copies, the Stjórn manuscripts and the Arabic ʿAṭṭār translation cannot be multiplied as independent inherited attestations.

**Warrant.** Their textual relationships are identifiable. Four witnesses can strengthen confidence in one scene’s transmitted wording without producing four independent origins. [S054,S056,S069;E21–E22,E24,E31]

**Limit.** Dependence within one cluster does not establish borrowing of the Hittite–Greek pairing. A local source relationship must not be generalized into an unsupported universal account.

## Thesis 8 — Distinctive sequences help when the actual actions and roles align

**Claim.** Ordered sequences can provide positive historical evidence, but only for the relationship they actually support.

**Warrant.** The Latin–Norse allegory and the Khotanese–Sanskrit question/answer alignment establish specific local relations. The Guntram sequence supports a narrative family beyond a single shared word. [S045,S062,S064–S065;E26–E28,E44]

**Limit.** Adjacent allegory does not source the preceding bodily scene. An animal leaving a mouth is not a woman withdrawing from beneath a head. Sequence evidence is an optional contribution to the larger reconstruction, not a compulsory template for every witness.

## Thesis 9 — Local composition is a real alternative, not an automatic veto

**Claim.** The existing controls show that knee-support usage and sleep/death diction were available separately in the relevant traditions.

**Warrant.** Hittite child-on-knees scenes lack sleep; Greek funerary sleep can lack knees; other traditions use arms, bosoms, garments or ground. These are concrete texts and representational controls, not an unsupported assertion that all such motifs are universal. [E03,E08–E18,E36–E37,E42]

**Limit.** Separate availability of the ingredients does not show that the pairing was first created locally. It leaves a competing account whose relative explanatory force must be judged against the positive lexical-role correspondence.

## Thesis 10 — Search inventories are not preference measurements

**Claim.** The corpus work does not establish a calibrated cross-language probability or a secure knee/sleep lexical-preference rate.

**Warrant.** The declared datasets contain unknown support, imperfect retrieval, non-independent copies and residual lexical ambiguity. The Persian and Irish counts do not define a complete matched-event denominator; Iranian unresolved words are not negatives. [S035,S040,S046,S048;E38,E45–E46,E51–E52]

**Limit.** This prevents a particular statistical argument. It does not prohibit qualitative reconstruction or make optional quantitative evidence a mandatory gate.

## Thesis 11 — Corrections strengthen the retained case

**Claim.** Welsh elbows, Druimenach’s elbow-ground sleep, unsupported Jātakastava sleep, the unrelated Plutarch locus and restored signs must remain excluded from stronger claims.

**Warrant.** Each correction follows a particular source or event-boundary check. Removing an attractive but false positive protects the historical meaning of the remaining evidence. [E32,E35,E43,E50;correction register]

**Limit.** Exclusion is bounded to the inspected text. It is not an assertion of absence throughout a language, genre or manuscript tradition.

## Thesis 12 — The historical outcome is a warranted proposal with a defined unresolved contrast

**Claim.** The cumulative record justifies proposing an inherited KNEE[SUPPORT]–SLEEP/DREAM lexical–semantic association.

**Warrant.** The early anchor combines cognate material and participant-role alignment; later positives and local transmission controls broaden and discipline the comparison. Normal historical inference is defeasible and does not require deductive uniqueness.

**Limit.** The record has not yet shown persuasive preference between inheritance of a paired convention and separate retention of knee-support usage and sleep vocabulary followed by local combination. No complete PIE sentence, obligatory narrative, shared phrase-specific metre or numerical probability follows.

## What may be stated without inflating the claim

The textual core is real. The principal Hittite and Greek sleep materials belong to the same inherited family. The supported-sleeper relation is a legitimate comparative object. Several later scenes are actual attestations. Several local transmission relationships are positively established. The larger inheritance proposal is therefore substantive and defensible.

The packet should not be described as “proof that PIE speakers conventionally said a fixed phrase about putting a head on knees.” It is a source-critical case for a proposed inherited association, with its strongest observations and remaining historical question explicitly separated. This is the most defensible thesis available from this record, not a promise of a stronger conclusion.
