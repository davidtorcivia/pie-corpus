# Where the research stands
## KNEE as bodily support and SLEEP/DREAM
### Consolidated assessment through round 49 · 28 October 2026

**Bottom line.** We have a substantive, source-controlled historical proposal, not a demonstrated Proto-Indo-European convention. The strongest early comparison remains Hittite Nerik KUB 36.89 reverse 56′–59′ and Greek Anthologia Palatina 7.170: inherited knee vocabulary, inherited *swep-family sleep vocabulary, and a person represented as sleeping while supported on knees. The comparison warrants proposing inherited lexical-semantic phraseology. The unresolved question is whether the pairing itself was conventional before the languages separated, or whether inherited support usage and sleep diction were combined within individual traditions. [B36: E01–E05; S001–S006]

The later work has made the evidence cleaner and the competing histories more concrete. It has not supplied another securely controlled, independent early branch that resolves that question. In particular, rounds 37–49 mainly added critical-edition checks, comparisons with other constructions, and transmission controls. Their value is not a growing count of independent positive attestations.

**What this document is.** This is a new synthesis of the authorized research record and the files actually available for assembly. It is not a new primary-source investigation, a peer-reviewed paper, or a claim of scholarly priority. Historical read scopes are retained as historical; no old corpus was rerun and no unavailable book was silently treated as read. The earlier paper through round 36 is preserved unchanged. [B36: methods and recovery records; R37–R49]

## 1. The claim we can defend

The proposed association is KNEE[SUPPORT]–SLEEP/DREAM. The knee supports the same participant who sleeps, dreams, or is described as asleep. It can be another person's knee or the sleeper's own knees. There is no requirement for an explicit head, a placement verb, an invariant sentence, a particular metre, withdrawal from under a head, or support by a second person.

This flexibility does not turn any nearby knee and sleep words into a positive. Kneeling in prayer, trembling knees, a knee injury within a dream, or a person who first sits on knees and later sleeps somewhere else are different relations. They may inform the comparison, but cannot be silently substituted for supported sleep.

The affirmative basis is stronger than two separate etymologies: the inherited words participate in a corresponding relationship. Conversely, cognate vocabulary and a corresponding relationship do not by themselves date the conventional pairing. Ordinary historical reconstruction is defeasible, so the existence of a possible alternative does not veto inheritance. Our present evidence nevertheless has not established a persuasive preference between the two specific histories above. [B36: Paper §§1–5; Defensible Theses 1–4]

## 2. What we have established about the strongest evidence

### Hittite: a real positive with important grammatical qualifications

KUB 36.89 reverse 56′–59′ combines written GI-NU-WA with a sleep derivative represented in the record as šuppariyanza and a pleasant-dream expression. The knee spelling contains no written final -aš. A form printed with an added ending must remain identifiable as an editorial intervention, not another spelling independently visible on the tablet. [B36: E01, E05; S001–S004]

The form ēšta allows competing person and BE/SIT analyses. Omission of reflexive -za can receive positive weight as a local archaic feature only under the specified second-person predicative-BE interpretation. It cannot be detached from that condition and then used as a general proof of archaic PIE syntax. The supporting figure also need not be the mother invoked elsewhere in the ritual. [B36: corrections 3–7; R34, R47]

The manuscript is classified as Young Hittite in the official concordance. This dates the copy's classification, not necessarily every expression it contains. Joined inventory fragments form one tablet. Duplicates for other ranges are useful, but none inspected in this record duplicates the knee–sleep clause itself. The original handcopy and Haas 1970, pp. 156–157, remain important unfinished checks. [B36: S002–S004; R34, R47]

### Greek: explicit knees and sleep, in a funerary poem

AP 7.170 ends with the child on his mother's knees in deep sleep: “ἐπὶ γούνων ματρὸς κοιμαθεὶς τὸν βαθὺν ὕπνον ἔχει.” The inherited sleep-family term is ὕπνον; the participle κοιμαθείς is a different formation. The poem concerns a three-year-old recovered from a well and is treated in the record as funerary/death-as-sleep, not as an unqualified ordinary nap. Attribution and the exact physiological moment of death are not resolved by this comparison. [B36: E02; S006]

The positive survives that qualification: a person represented as asleep is supported on knees. The Hittite and Greek grammars, genres and supporting relationships differ. No single inflected PIE sentence, common case ending, or knee-associated metre has been recovered. Later manuscript copies of the epigram are witnesses to one poem, not additional independent occurrences. [B36: Paper §§3–5]

### Why this is still the centre of the case

These two passages provide the clearest early alignment of cognate lexical material and the supported-sleeper role. Later positives widen the semantic and historical record; they do not automatically have the same chronological or genealogical weight. The strongest case is therefore cumulative and differentiated, not a tally of every occurrence labelled “lap” in translation.

## 3. Later positives and the transmission discoveries

### Irish: an explicit knee-supported sleep episode

The prose preface to the Félire Óengusso has the invitation “tabair do chenn for mo glún & cotail”: put your head on my knee and sleep. The boy later reports the same posture and sleep. The episode connects the rest with miraculous learning and recognition of Óengus. Four selected manuscript witnesses are recorded as checked in the governing handoff. They corroborate one story, not four independently inherited formulas. [B36: E30–E31; S068–S070; R19]

This is a genuine Celtic semantic positive. It is not automatically a *swep-root positive: cotlud belongs to different sleep diction. Nor may the prose scene simply be assigned the date of the saint or the older metrical martyrology. Its identification and manuscript control are gains of this project; the underlying published story was not newly discovered by us.

### Persian and its Arabic translation: self-support and a consequential variant

The ʿAṭṭār material records self-supported knee postures associated with sleep or dreaming. In Nicholson I, p. 316, lines 13–15, the printed affirmative bi-xuft contrasts with apparatus C's na-xuft, “did not sleep.” The subsequent dream material must remain visible alongside the negation. [B36: E19–E22; S047, S054]

The Arabic translation's corresponding Yūsuf passage explicitly combines denied sleep, the head on the person's own knees, drowsiness, and a dream. This is useful evidence for the history of one narrative, not a new independent Semitic confirmation of PIE inheritance. Its copy date is not the date of the Persian wording or of the translation. Earlier Persian target leaves remain unread. [B36: corrections 13–18]

The Sām-nāma positive is securely controlled at Ben-Shahi II, printed p. 313. That is an inspected twentieth-century edition, not verification of Rūyānī's apparatus or proof that the exact verse survives from the Middle Ages. [B36: E23; S049–S050]

### Norse and Latin: real scenes, uneven source relationships

Selected Stjórn and Sörla manuscript passages confirm knee-supported sleep and associated actions. In Stjórn, the Samson sequence is part of a biblical translation tradition. An ordered Latin–Norse allegory comparison establishes a source-family relationship for neighboring interpretation, but does not by itself identify the immediate source of the physical sleep-and-withdrawal sequence. Directionality among the later Norse scenes remains open. [B36: E24–E26; S056–S062]

The Latin Guntram tradition supplies another knee/lap-supported sleep sequence with distinctive events. Later retellings strengthen confidence in a transmitted narrative family rather than adding independent Indo-European branches. A narrated year is not a date for the extant words. [B36: E27–E29; S064–S067]

## 4. What rounds 37–49 added

### Established knee-support constructions without sleep

Direct critical editions sharpened the Hittite child-on-knees material. KBo 17.61 contains a child, knees and placement, with restored opening clitics and uncertainty about the possessor and preceding pronoun. Ullikummi has separate transfers to Kumarbi's and Enlil's knees, followed by a different shoulder-support action. The witnesses and joined fragments were untangled rather than counted repeatedly. No knee-placement clause inspected there contains sleep. [R37–R39]

Homeric and Norse controls extend this comparison. Odyssey 19.399–409 combines infant knee placement and naming; Iliad 9.485–495 combines knees and feeding. Haraldz saga §§21 and 40, in the specified Finnur Jónsson edition, uses knee-setting in naming/fosterage and royal status. These are substantive uses of knees in social relations, but their separate historical explanations remain open. They do not by themselves establish an inherited child-adoption formula, much less a knee–sleep formula. [R41–R42]

### A close Homeric association with an actual support change

Iliad 22.500–504 is especially informative. Astyanax is fed on Hector's knees; when sleep overtakes him, he sleeps in his nurse's arms in bed. The same child, knees, and inherited sleep vocabulary occur very close together, but the physical support changes. This is a close contextual comparison, not another unambiguous knee-supported sleeper. [R41]

### Dream diction recurring independently of knees

KUB 36.90 describes the Storm-god of Nerik being roused from pleasant dreams without a knee term in the preserved clause. Its relationship with KUB 36.89 makes this a close local comparison, not a remote thematic resemblance. Bird language near KUB 36.89's knee–sleep lines supplies an exact contextual lead; continuity through the intervening lines still needs checking. [R43]

The latest substantive gain is the near-verbatim complaint across related prayers CTH 372, 373 and 374: at night, in the speaker's bed, no pleasant dream takes hold of the speaker. The critical editions reveal different preservation states: the dream noun is written in one text and mostly restored in another. This is genuine recurring Hittite prayer diction, not three independent branches. Nor is absence of a pleasant dream necessarily inability to sleep. [R49]

The associated life/death rhetoric has a specific Sumerian comparison discussed by Metcalf. That observation remains narrower than a proof that the negative pleasant-dream clause itself was translated from Sumerian. The original Sumerian target and full relevant studies remain unfinished checks. [R49 follow-up]

### Restoration and false-co-occurrence controls

The bilingual annals of Ḫattušili preserve an Akkadian lap expression where the proposed Hittite knee word lies in a gap. A plausible bilingual restoration is not a written Hittite knee token. In Appu, the adult's bed-sleep and the newborn's later knee placement are separate events. In Hedammu, trembling knees and the monster's sleep concern different passages and roles. [R40, R45]

The Telipinu edition preserves a sleeping predicate but restores the following waking question. CTH 390's knees/hands belong to stages of a binding ritual; the duplicate hand signs are not fully preserved. These controls prevent translations, restorations and nearby words from becoming fabricated target attestations. [R44, R46, R48]

## 5. The corpus work and what its counts mean

The earlier project corrected retrieval gaps and examined substantial bounded samples. The record reports two Ṛgveda editions with 10,552 matching stanza IDs; the Khotanese audit acquired 1,648 advertised pages containing 201,948 tagged tokens; the Persian screen covered 96 files and 6,432 paragraphs; the Irish screen covered 36 textual bodies. These are documented acquisition and screening scopes, not claims that every passage was semantically translated. [B36: Corpus_Audit; S035, S040, S047–S048, S068]

Nineteen confirmed Khotanese knee contexts supplied no supported sleeper, but unresolved words and family-only forms remain outside that conclusion. The Avestan audit likewise retains uncertainties. The Persian data contain many unspecified supports, and the Irish retrieval process demonstrably missed some candidates. Unknown support is not evidence of non-knee support. No valid lexical-preference estimate or inheritance probability has been extracted from these hit sets. [B36: Corpus_Audit and corrections 30–39]

This remains a useful research output: a bounded, reproducible screening history with explicit exclusions and unresolved cases is more informative than an unsupported declaration that a corpus contains no examples. The current archive preserves the authored results, not the full underlying downloaded corpora or a fresh rerun of those audits.

## 6. What is novel—and what is not

### Project-level contributions that can be claimed

**An integrated comparison.** The dossier brings the Hittite–Greek lexical-role alignment into one argument with Persian, Celtic, Germanic and Latin positives, explicit dependencies, alternative support expressions and source-critical controls. This is the principal candidate contribution of the paper.

**A more reliable evidence base.** The project distinguishes actual knee words from translated “lap,” affirmative sleep from negation plus drowsiness, written signs from supplied ones, and one transmitted scene from multiple copies. Examples include the Persian polarity variant, the rejected Welsh elbow translation, joined Ullikummi fragments, and the Hattušili restoration. These are corrections and verifications in this project's record; they are not claims that no earlier specialist noticed them.

**Corpus and witness audit products.** The controlled searches, expanded lemma coverage, role-by-role passage classifications, witness alignments, read-scope ledgers and preserved checkpoints are concrete research outputs. Many individual sources were already published. An original analytical dataset can still be useful without the claim that its component texts are new.

**A sharper historical question.** The work now compares inheritance of the paired convention with inheritance of the component support and sleep usages followed by local combination. The newer controls give the latter account actual textual substance, but do not settle which history better explains the target pairing.

### Claims of novelty that have not been established

We have not established that this project is the first to propose the Hittite–Greek association, the first to recognize any individual ancient passage, or the first to study knee placement, dream formulae or these literary translations. Important exact scholarship, including the two Massetti chapters, remains unread. A field-wide priority claim would therefore be premature.

We have not recovered a new tablet, edited a previously unpublished manuscript, proved a fixed PIE sentence, obtained a statistical probability of inheritance, or produced external peer review. “Newly checked here,” “new to this dossier,” and “new to scholarship” are different assertions. The archive supports the first two in specified cases; it does not certify the third.

## 7. What remains unresolved and where effort is most valuable

**The principal Hittite text.** Original KUB 36.89 handcopy and Haas 1970, pp. 156–157, remain the highest-value checks for the exact writing, case alternatives, supporter and person/construction. The surrounding bird and recall passage should be read continuously without assuming sleep means death. A late copy can transmit an older expression, but the age of that expression must be argued separately.

**The scholarship and earliest target witnesses.** Full Massetti 2022 and 2023 chapters are needed to place the argument accurately within existing literature. Persian Leiden/BL target leaves and the Rūyānī apparatus matter to dating and variants. Grønlie and the relevant Stjórn apparatus matter to local transmission. These gaps are recorded, not concealed by substituting abstracts, previews or adjacent material.

**Discrimination rather than accumulation.** Another non-knee childcare scene is unlikely, by itself, to resolve the central historical question. Greater value lies in an independently early supported-sleeper expression, a demonstrable path of textual dependence, a better local comparison of lexical choices, or a closer examination of the strongest existing anchors. None is a compulsory gate to proposing inheritance; each could change its relative evidential support.

The immediate conclusion is therefore neither “proved” nor “nothing found.” We have identified a substantive inherited-association proposal, verified a considerable later attestation and transmission record, and removed several ways of overstating it. Conventional PIE paired selection and its precise historical depth remain open.
