# Phase 8c: reader-agent parallel discovery — pre-registration

Written 2026-10-23, before any packet is built or read. Approved by David the same day ("Do that new experiment … it definitely needs an agent to read and 'mentally' compare across context").

## Why
Counting co-occurrences (8a, rounds 1–3) finds known Graeco-Aryan formulas above chance but adds nothing outside that core, and found no new formula (docs/research/8a-confirmatory.md). Inherited phraseology often survives with **semantic drift** (a cognate word in a shifted sense), **lexical renewal** (one member replaced by a synonym), or **reshaped syntax**, and many parallels are attested once. Those are invisible to counting. A careful reader can see them. The danger is the reverse: a capable reader can find "parallels" anywhere (apophenia), and a language model may recall famous formulas from training. This design measures the false-discovery rate directly instead of trusting claims.

## Design
1. **Themes (16).** Situations, not formulas: (1) slaying a serpent or monster, (2) cattle raid and recovery, (3) funeral and the road of the dead, (4) oath and its breaking, (5) kindling and praising fire, (6) the horse and the chariot, (7) Sky and Earth as parents, (8) the dawn, (9) the sun's course, (10) healing and binding charms, (11) the poet and his craft, (12) gift and counter-gift, (13) guest and host, (14) battle and the warrior's fury, (15) drinking and feasting, (16) rain, storm and thunder. Each theme is given as an English concept list; its **seed roots** are the lexicon lexemes whose glosses match the concept list, and their roots (a deterministic script; the list of seeds is frozen with the packets).
2. **Passages.** From the local corpora per branch: Indo-Iranian (Rigveda; Atharvaveda Śaunaka/Paippalāda from DCS; Avesta), Greek (Homer; GLAUx poetry ≤ 400 BC), Germanic (Poetic Edda; Old English poetry), Anatolian (Hittite), Italic (Latin LASLA; Umbrian), Baltic (Latvian dainas), Celtic (Old Irish, where usable). Bible translations (Gothic, OCS, Armenian) are excluded as parallel sources (translation literature). A passage is a verse line or stanza with one unit of context either side. It qualifies for a theme if it contains ≥ 2 distinct seed roots (round-3 lemma→PIE mappings). Per branch and theme: the top 30 by number of distinct seed roots, plus 6 random qualifying passages; branches with < 5 qualifying passages are omitted from that packet.
3. **Packet format.** Per passage: corpus locus; the text; per content word its lemma, the mapped PIE etymon (or "unmapped"), and the lexicon's gloss. No translation is supplied (readers read the languages); no theme label; no source books.
4. **Real and decoy packets.** 16 real packets (one per theme, all its branches) and 8 decoy packets. A decoy keeps the packet structure but each branch block comes from a *different* theme (a random derangement across themes, so no two branch blocks share a theme). True inherited parallels between decoy blocks are possible (themes overlap) but should be rarer; the decoy claim rate is therefore an upper bound on the false-discovery rate. Packets get random ids; the key (id → real/decoy, theme(s)) is written to `data/audits/keys/8c-key.json` (gitignored) and seen by no reader or reviewer.
5. **Readers.** One reader agent per 3 packets (mixed real/decoy, randomized), strongest available model. Instruction: find inherited parallels — passages in ≥ 2 branches whose wording continues a common phrase — allowing drift and renewal, but naming for every claim: the passages (loci), the words in each, their etyma, what is cognate exactly, what has drifted or been renewed and why that is plausible, what syntax is shared; and a confidence (high/medium/low). Readers may and should report "no parallel". Readers must not consult the books (West, Watkins, Schmitt, Matasović, EWAia, the Leiden dictionaries) or the 8a formula files; they may consult the corpora (to widen context around a cited passage) and the lexicon.
6. **Blind adversarial review.** Every claim (from real and decoy packets, shuffled, without packet ids or reader confidence) goes to a skeptical reviewer agent (not a reader), who checks the cited passages in the corpora and grades: **accepted** (genuine shared inherited wording: at least one exact cognate pair in a congruent collocation, plus a second element cognate or a defensible drift/renewal, in comparable context), **weak** (defensible but not compelling), **reject** (coincidence, wrong etymology, generic language, misread passage).
7. **Novelty check.** Each accepted claim is checked by a separate agent against the literature we hold: West 2007, Watkins 1995, Schmitt 1967, Matasović 1996, the 8a eval set and formulas.jsonl, EWAia and the Leiden dictionaries (which cite phrasal parallels in places). Outcome: **known** (the parallel is stated there), **partly known** (the words are compared but not the phrase), or **not found** (= candidate new).

## Measures and success criteria (fixed now)
- **Primary:** accepted claims per real packet (r) vs per decoy packet (d). Success if r ≥ 2·d and a one-sided permutation test over packet labels (10,000 permutations, seed 0) gives p < 0.05. If d = 0, success needs r > 0 with p < 0.05.
- **Secondary:** the same for accepted + weak; the reviewer's acceptance rate of claims from real vs decoy packets; the reader-confidence calibration (accepted share by confidence level).
- **Recovery:** accepted claims that match the 8a eval set or formulas.jsonl, reported separately and labelled *possibly recalled from training*, not as evidence of discovery ability.
- **Candidate new parallels:** accepted claims with novelty "not found" (and "partly known" separately). Each is written up with its passages and argument as a *candidate* needing expert confirmation; we have no expert, so none is called a finding.
- **Stop rule:** one run. No re-reading of packets with changed instructions to raise the count. Errors found later go to an erratum.

## Reporting
docs/research/8c-results.md: the numbers above with packet-level tables, the permutation p, every accepted claim (real or decoy, labelled after unblinding), and the candidate-new list. Packets, claims and reviews are committed (no daughter text beyond the cited words; NC corpora are quoted only by locus).

## Stage B: the discovery run (added 2026-10-23, before any Stage A packet was read)
David: the design above is the validation; "for the real run we want to use as large of a corpus as possible." Stage A (above) measures readers' false-discovery rate on a controlled sample; Stage B is the exhaustive run, interpreted with Stage A's numbers and with its own embedded decoys.

- **Gate:** Stage B starts only after Stage A is scored. If Stage A fails its success criterion, Stage B still runs only if David decides so, and its claims are then reported with the Stage A result as the headline caveat.
- **Corpora: everything local and readable.** Indo-Iranian: Rigveda, Atharvaveda Śaunaka and Paippalāda, Avesta. Greek: Homer and all GLAUx poetry and prose to 300 BC. Germanic: Poetic Edda, Old English poetry. Anatolian: all of Hittite (TLHdig). Italic: Latin (LASLA), Umbrian. Baltic: Latvian dainas. Celtic: Old Irish (what is usable). Slavic: the Igor Tale and early Old Russian from TOROT (research-only licence: read, never copied out). Bible translations excluded as parallel sources.
- **Retrieval (generous by design; readers judge).** Every unit (stanza, line window, sentence) of every corpus is a pivot. Candidate partners: units in *other branches* sharing ≥ 2 PIE roots at root-group level (so drifted cognates connect), scored by summed inverse root frequency; top partners per pivot above a fixed score threshold, merged into comparison bundles (one pivot + up to 6 partners from ≥ 1 other branch, overlapping bundles merged). The threshold and caps are frozen by the bundle builder before any bundle is read, and the full bundle list is committed.
- **Embedded decoys.** 20% of bundles, chosen at random, have their partners replaced by frequency- and length-matched units from other branches sharing no seed-root overlap with the pivot beyond chance level; readers and reviewers are blind to this. The key is gitignored as in Stage A.
- **Reading, review, novelty:** as in Stage A (sections 5–7), readers ~20 bundles each.
- **Measures:** as in Stage A, per bundle instead of per packet: accepted claims per real bundle vs per decoy bundle (permutation test, one-sided, 10,000 permutations, seed 0); every accepted claim listed; candidate-new list with novelty status. Claims in Stage B are reported with the false-discovery estimate from both Stage A and Stage B's decoys.
- **Cost estimate, stated in advance:** about 2,000–4,000 bundles, 100–200 reader runs plus review, on the order of 30–60M tokens.

## Reader capacity and search (added 2026-10-23, before any packet was read)
David: "with each agent holding as much as they can to widen search."
- **Stage B slabs.** Readers use the largest-context model available and each receives a slab of as much passage data as fits (target ~500–700k tokens): a whole pivot section (e.g. one Rigveda maṇḍala, a block of Homer, a Hittite text group) with its partner pools from all other branches, instead of ~20 small bundles. Embedded decoys (20% of pivots' partner pools) are mixed into every slab.
- **Free corpus search, both stages.** Readers may search every local corpus (via the loaders and mappings, or python3 -I on raw/; never executing anything there) to widen a lead: other occurrences of the roots, other branches, wider context. Books and 8a formula files stay off limits.
- **Anchoring rule, both stages.** A claim must include at least two passages from the reader's packet or slab, from different branches; witnesses found by search are added as support and listed separately. (Otherwise a decoy reader could report a real parallel found elsewhere, and the decoy rate would stop measuring false discovery.)
- **Known limitation.** Stage A packets are smaller than Stage B slabs, so Stage A's false-discovery estimate transfers only approximately; Stage B's embedded decoys measure it under the real conditions and are the primary control for Stage B.

## Stage B build: parameters and deviations (recorded 2026-10-23, before any slab was read)
- **Floor:** 90th percentile of best-partner scores (12.35), frozen in data/research/8c/stageB/params.json; a size rule only.
- **Size:** 10,236 bundles, 2,048 embedded decoys (20.0%), 50 slabs of 296k-700k tokens (32.0M; ~41.6M with reader overhead), within the pre-stated estimate.
- **Retrieval vs chance:** against a null with root sets shuffled within each corpus, the real texts do not have more high-scoring pivots (at the floor: 19,527 real vs 23,983 null). Retrieval is therefore a frequency-driven sample of root-sharing pairs, as intended; the embedded decoys are the control.
- **Deviations:** the Edda edition's notes section (modern Icelandic commentary, 1,012 units; included in Stage A) is dropped; the *Thesaurus Palaeohibernicus* OCR is excluded (unlemmatized); Kroonen was not used for mapping. Mapping errata from Stage A are fixed (data/research/formulas/mapping-overrides.json).
- **Coverage:** Greek prose is 54% of pivots; Hittite has 7 pivot bundles, Umbrian 0, Old Irish 3; Old Russian mapping covers 32% of content tokens. Not re-weighted (the build was frozen); reading order puts the poetry slabs first, which affects only the order, not what is read.
- **Incident (wave 1, disclosed by reader RB020):** readers shared the session scratchpad; another reader's script overwrote RB020's helper script and RB020 briefly saw ~15 bundles of slab S-42da (read by RB048, which produced no claims). RB020 disregarded them and recorded this in its done line. From wave 2, readers write scratch files only under a per-reader prefix.
- **Incident (wave 1, disclosed by reader RB002):** the same shared-scratchpad overwrite; RB002 saw one section of another slab, used none of it, and moved to a private folder.
- **Incident (wave 1, disclosed by reader RB019):** the same overwrite; RB019 briefly saw part of slab S-42da (no claims came from S-42da) and disregarded it.
- **Incident (wave 1, disclosed by reader RB001):** the same overwrite; RB001 briefly saw two bundles of slab S-42da. All four disclosed exposures concern S-42da, which produced no claims.
