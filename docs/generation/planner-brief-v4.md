# Tier 3 planner brief, v4: connected texts (`prompt_id: tier3-planner-v4`)

You write PLANS for whole PIE TEXTS: stories, hymns, prayers, law codes, dialogues. Each text is a connected passage, as long as it needs to be and no longer (typically 8–30 clauses). **The caps scale with the batch: the per-lexeme cap is max(6, 0.5% of the batch's tokens), so longer texts raise it. Never shorten a text to fit a cap; widen the vocabulary instead (t017 wrote 23-word texts believing the cap forced it), with its own characters, topic and line of thought. **Never pad** to reach a length: a flat filler clause is worse than a shorter text (t004 review). **You never write PIE.** A plan is English plus lexeme ids, roles and features; a deterministic engine (`pie/realize.py`; read its docstring for the full schema) produces the PIE.

The goal is **sophisticated, accurate prose**: texts that read like the oldest Indo-European literature (the Rigveda, Homer, the Hittite laws, the Avesta), not like a list of grammar-book sentences. Accuracy comes first: every plan must say exactly what its English says.

Do not read anything under `corpus/` or `raw/`, or earlier plan files.

## Format
One JSON object per line, one line per TEXT:
`{"plan_id": "<batch>-0001", "genre": "...", "theme": ["..."], "title": "short English title", "plan": {"english": "the whole text in English", "clauses": [...]}}`

Clause fields: `type` ("verbal" default, "nominal", "np": see below), `order` ("SOV" default, or "VSO" verb-initial), `conj` ("kʷe" = "and", "wē" = "or", joining this clause to the previous one), `neg` (ne; with IMP or OPT it is mē: "do not!", "may it not …"), `particle` ("nu" = "now, then", clause-initial; "ge" = emphatic "indeed", enclitic; "yod" = h₁yód "if, when", opening a subordinate clause, e.g. the condition of a law), `break` (this clause starts a new sentence), `question` (its sentence ends with "?"), `english` (OPTIONAL, below).
**Per-sentence English (optional, new).** On the clause that starts a sentence (the first clause, and every clause with `break`), you may add `"english"`: that one sentence's English, exactly as it appears in the plan's `english`. Give it on every sentence-initial clause or on none; the values in order, joined with spaces, are the plan's `english` (quotation marks and "User:"/"Model:" turn markers may be left out). The engine ignores the field; the chat export uses it to pair each PIE sentence with its English exactly (`pie/export_chat.py` `align_english`), instead of re-splitting the running English, which fails on quotations, semicolons and "Then he said:" frames. Write it for dialogues above all. Example: `"english": "Who are you? I am a shepherd; my name is Wolf."`, `"clauses": [{"english": "Who are you?", "question": true, "slots": [...]}, {"break": true, "english": "I am a shepherd; my name is Wolf.", "slots": [...]}, {"conj": "kʷe", "slots": [...]}]`. A clause that continues a sentence (no `break`) carries no `english`.
Slot fields: `lex_id`, `role` (SUBJ OBJ IO INS LOC ABL POSS VOC PRED GOAL MOD APPOS NP ADV V), `num` (SG DU PL), `head` (MOD, POSS and APPOS: index of the noun slot it belongs to), `before` (APPOS before its head), `enclitic` (pronoun slots), `gender` (pronoun slots used on their own), `and` (-kʷe after this word: "X Y-and" = "X and Y". **Put `and` on the LAST conjunct**, never the first: cows and sheep = gʷṓs h₂ówins-kʷe (Lat. senatus populusque). The engine moves a mark found only on an earlier conjunct of the same role, t014 review; a predicate adjective with a dropped subject takes the verb's number), `participle` and `dep` (below).
Verb slot: `{"lex_id": ..., "role": "V", "verb": {"tense": "PRS|PST|AOR|PRF", "mood": "IND|IMP|SBJV|OPT", "voice": "ACT|MID", "person": 1|2|3, "num": "SG|PL"}}`.

**Numerals and adverbs (new).** Numerals are closed ids in the slice's `closed` list (`closed:numerals/2` … `closed:numerals/indeclinable/10`), used as a MOD with a `head`; the head noun takes the dual with "two" and the plural with three and more. Adverbs and adpositions are in the slice's `adverbs` list, used as role ADV. An adposition's object is its own slot with `dep` = the ADV slot and role GOAL (toward), LOC (in), ABL (from), INS (with) or IO (for); it stands before the adposition: "toward them" = tóns próti.

**Pronouns and deictics (new).** Four gendered closed ids, each usable as a MOD with a `head` ("this earth", "that heaven") or on its own with a `gender`: `closed:anaphoric_i` = "he, she, it" for someone or something just mentioned, with no pointing (Lat. *is, ea, id*); `closed:demonstrative_so` = "that one, the", the general demonstrative and the correlative of a relative clause; `closed:deictic_ki` = "this (here)", near the speaker; `closed:deictic_eno` = "that (yonder)", far from the speaker, for contrast with "this" ("this earth … that heaven"). The anaphoric has no dual forms: for two referents use `closed:demonstrative_so`.

**Participles (new).** A MOD slot may hold a verb with `"participle": {"voice": "ACT"}` (or "MID"): it agrees with its head noun. A slot with `"dep": <index of the participle slot>` is the participle's own argument (its object, instrument, …) and takes the case of its own role. Example, "the man carrying the load saw the horses":
`[{"lex_id": "<man>", "role": "SUBJ"}, {"lex_id": "<carry>", "role": "MOD", "head": 0, "participle": {"voice": "ACT"}}, {"lex_id": "<load>", "role": "OBJ", "dep": 1}, {"lex_id": "<horse>", "role": "OBJ", "num": "PL"}, {"lex_id": "<see>", "role": "V", ...}]`. Use participles for background action, the way Vedic and Homeric narrative does ("having seized the cattle, he drove them home" → a participle + a main verb). The perfect participle is not available.

**Comparison: "swifter", "swiftest", "swifter than X" (new).** Put `"degree": "CMP"` (comparative) or `"degree": "SUP"` (superlative) on an adjective slot: MOD, PRED, APPOS, or a noun role with a `gender` ("the swiftest one"). The engine builds the form from the root (HéHḱus → HéHḱyōs, HéHḱistos; swéh₂dus → swéh₂dyōs, swéh₂distos; méǵh₂s → méǵyōs, méǵistos). Only primary adjectives have these forms: u-stems, -ro- adjectives, root-accented ones like néwos and sénos, and old root adjectives like méǵh₂s. Participles, -tó- adjectives ('given', 'seen'), -yo- adjectives ('heavenly'), compounds and the like are rejected with a reason; say "more" another way there. The standard ("than the wolf") is its own slot, role ABL, with `"dep"` = the index of the comparative slot; it stands right before the comparative. "The horse is swifter than the wolf" =
`[{"lex_id": "<horse>", "role": "SUBJ"}, {"lex_id": "<wolf>", "role": "ABL", "dep": 2}, {"lex_id": "<swift>", "role": "PRED", "degree": "CMP"}]` with `"type": "nominal"` → h₁éḱwos wĺ̥kʷōd HéHḱyōs. "Than you" is the pronoun in ABL (twéd). With a superlative, "of the horses" is role POSS (num PL) with `"dep"` = the superlative slot. The older way to say "the greatest" is just as good, and better in hymns: the positive with a genitive plural ("divine among goddesses"). Use the slot field instead of the old comparative lexemes (méǵyōs, swéh₂dyōs, pléh₁yōs): it covers any primary adjective, and the tokens say what they are (`degree_status`: the comparative is "nuclear-IE", missing in Anatolian; the superlative "late-IE").

**Pronouns with adpositions: "with me", "among us", "between us" (new).** A 1st/2nd-person pronoun can be an adposition's object like any noun: its own slot with `dep` = the ADV slot. "With me / with you / with us" = ADV ḱóm (pie-lex-kjom) + the pronoun with role INS → méǵʰye ḱóm, tébʰye ḱóm, n̥sméy ḱóm. "Among us" = ADV médʰi + the pronoun with role LOC → n̥sméy médʰi. The pronouns have no instrumental or locative (none is reconstructable), so under an adposition the engine uses the dative and marks the token `case_fallback`; this is expected. Without an adposition a pronoun cannot be INS or LOC ("by me" is rejected; rephrase). "Between us" = ADV h₁entér + pronoun role GOAL (accusative: n̥smé h₁entér). "From me" = ABL (méd h₂epó). Never mark such a pronoun `enclitic`. Nouns keep the instrumental: "with the wolf" = wĺ̥kʷoh₁ ḱóm. (docs/morphology-comparison-and-comitative.md)

**Compounds (new).** A slot may hold a compound built by the engine from two lexemes: `{"lex_id": "compound", "compound": {"first": F, "second": S, "type": "bahuvrihi"}, "role": "MOD", "head": k}`. A **bahuvrihi** is an adjective "having (a) F S" ("of good fame", "with swift horses", "ill-minded", "horseless") and is used exactly like an adjective: MOD with a `head`, PRED, or with a `gender` as a noun ("the one of good fame"). First members F: `closed:prefix/h1su` "good", `closed:prefix/dus` "bad, ill", `closed:prefix/n` "un-, -less, without", `closed:prefix/sm` "one, the same", `closed:numerals/2|3|4` "two-, three-, four-", or an adjective (thematic -os or u-stem -us) or a noun from the slice. Second members S: a noun marked `compound_second: true` in the slice (s-stem neuters like ḱléwos "fame", ménos "mind", and thematic nouns like h₁éḱwos "horse"). Examples: h1su + ḱléwos = h₁suḱléwēs "of good fame" (Ved. suśrávas-, Gk εὐκλεής); dus + ménos = dusménēs "ill-minded, hostile" (Gk δυσμενής); h1su + h₁éḱwos = h₁suh₁éḱwos "having good horses" (Ved. suáśva-, Gk εὔιππος); swift + horse = "with swift horses". A **determinative** (`"type": "determinative"`) is a noun "an F S" with S's gender ("new-earth"); first member an adjective or a noun, never a prefix; it takes a noun role, not MOD. Use compounds for inherited epithets of gods and heroes, sparingly (one or two per text). The checker rejects unsupported combinations with a reason: don't force them. Don't build a compound when the slice already has the word (ń̥dʰgʷʰitos "imperishable", n̥mr̥tós "immortal").

**Question words (new).** Closed ids, used as role `ADV`, fronted automatically like kʷís/kʷós: `closed:interrogative_adv/why` kʷíd (Ved. kím, Lat. quid, Gk τί), `/when` kʷóm (Lat. quom, Goth. ƕan), `/where` kúdʰe (Ved. kúha, OCS kъde), `/how` kʷíh₁ (Lat. quī). `closed:interrogative_adv/how_many` kʷóti (Lat. quot, Ved. káti) and its answers `closed:correlative_adv/so_many` tóti (Lat. tot) and `closed:correlative_adv/as_many` h₁yóti (Ved. yáti) are a MOD with a `head` (the noun takes the plural: "how many horses?" = kʷóti h₁éḱwons …?) or an ADV on their own. "Which of the two?" is the adjective kʷóteros (Gk πότερος, Ved. katará-), a MOD or, with a `gender`, a pronoun (a lexeme: the engine does not front it). "Who/what" stay `closed:interrogative_kwi`/`_kwo`. Set `question: true`. Never use kʷíd "what" as an OBJ for "why".

**Personal names (new).** Don't name people with plain nouns ("Wolf", "Oak"). Inherited names are dithematic compounds, the same bahuvrihis as the epithets: give the compound slot a noun role (SUBJ, OBJ, VOC, APPOS …) and the bearer's `gender`. Ready-made (first + second, `type: "bahuvrihi"`; masculine/feminine nominative):
- `closed:prefix/h1su` + ḱléwos `pie-lex-kjlewos` = h₁suḱléwēs "Of-Good-Fame": Ved. Suśrávas-, Av. Haosravah-, Gk Εὐκλῆς (West 2007 ch. 10).
- `pie-lex-h1wesus` + ḱléwos = h₁wesuḱléwēs "Of-Goodly-Fame": Illyr. Vescleves-, Ved. vásu-śravas- (Fortson §20.15).
- `pie-lex-h1sntyos` + ḱléwos = h₁sn̥tyóḱlewēs "Of-True-Fame": Ved. Satyáśravas-; Gk Ἐτεοκλῆς has the same sense (West ch. 10).
- `pie-lex-deywos` + ḱléwos = deywóḱlewēs "Fame-from-the-Gods": Ved. Deváśravas-; Gk Θεοκλῆς same sense (West ch. 10).
- `pie-lex-deywos` + ǵénh₁os `pie-lex-gjenh1os-2` = deywóǵenh₁ēs "Of-Divine-Race": Gaul. Divogena, Gk Διογένης (West ch. 10).
- `closed:prefix/h1su` + h₁éḱwos `pie-lex-h1ekjwos` = h₁suh₁éḱwos (f. h₁suh₁éḱweh₂) "Of-Good-Horses": Av. Hvaspa-, Ved. Sauvaśva-, Gk Εὔιππος (West ch. 10).
- `pie-lex-h2nexr` + ménos `pie-lex-menos` = h₂nr̥ménēs "Man-Minded": Av. Nərəmanah-, Gk Ἀνδρομένης, Ved. nṛmáṇas- (West ch. 10).
- `closed:prefix/h1su` + ménos = h₁suménēs "Kindly": Gk Εὐμένης; εὐμενής = Ved. sumánas- = Av. humanah- (West ch. 2).
- `closed:prefix/dus` + ménos = dusménēs "Ill-Minded", for a villain: an epithet (Gk δυσμενής, Ved. durmanas-, Av. dušmanah-), not an attested name.
Both members must be in your slice (slices built from 2026-10-28 on always include them). Use one name per character and keep it. Ḱlewo- as a first member (Gk Κλεο-), Méǵh₂-ḱléwos and Dyēus- names (Gk Διο-κλῆς) are not built: the engine has no compositional form for them.

**Greetings and thanks (new).** No PIE greeting or thanks formula is reconstructible: don't invent one. Build them as the daughter texts do, from words in your slice (slices built from 2026-10-28 on include all of these):
- An address and a command: VOC + IMP "come!" gʷémt `pie-lex-gwemt` (AOR IMP 2SG gʷm̥dʰí, Ved. ā́ gahi, Gk βῆθι), or "be well/whole!" h₁ésti IMP + PRED kéh₂ilos `pie-lex-keh2ilos` (Goth. hails, OE wes hāl: Germanic only).
- A wish (OPT): "may you be good/fortunate" h₁ésti OPT 2SG + PRED h₁wésus `pie-lex-h1wesus`; "may the gods give you good things" deywós PL + `closed:personal/2` enclitic IO + OBJ h₁wésus (`gender: "n"`, `num: "PL"`: "good things") + déh₃t `pie-lex-deh3t` AOR OPT (Od. 24.402 θεοὶ δέ τοι ὄλβια δοῖεν).
- Welcome: VOC gʰóstis `pie-lex-ghostis` ("guest") with MOD priHós `pie-lex-priHos` ("dear guest"), and "be dear to us" (h₁ésti IMP/OPT + PRED priHós + IO `closed:personal/1` PL; Od. 1.123 χαῖρε, ξεῖνε· παρ᾽ ἄμμι φιλήσεαι).
- Thanks are praise of the giver: gʷr̥néH- `pie-lex-gwrneHti` "praise", or the formula "giver of good things" déh₃tōr + POSS h₁wésus (Ved. dātā́ vásūnām).

**Verbless sentences, noun phrases, apposition (new).** The engine never adds a copula: write what the daughter texts have, not what English needs.
- **Nominal sentence** (`"type": "nominal"`, no V slot): SUBJ + PRED, e.g. "the father (is) good" (Hitt. *attaš aššuš*), "you (are) Varuṇa" (Ved. *tváṃ váruṇa*), "I (am) Darius" (OPers. *adam Dārayavauš*). PRED is a noun or adjective (nominative; an adjective agrees with the subject). The subject must be overt (a pronoun if need be). Order: subject first, predicate last (`order: "VSO"` = predicate first); `neg` puts *ne* before the predicate; other roles (VOC, IO, LOC, ADV, POSS …) are allowed, OBJ/GOAL are not. Use it for timeless, generic statements, identifications, proverbs and hymn epithets in the present. Use a verbal clause with h₁ésti instead for a past, a wish or command, emphasis on being, and existence ("there is a king"): the copula is optional only in the present.
- **Noun-phrase utterance** (`"type": "np"`, optional `"case"`: "NOM" default, "VOC" for addresses): a bare noun phrase as a whole sentence: a title, an exclamation, an address, a line of a catalogue of epithets. Its head slot has role `NP` (several NPs with `and` for a coordinated list) and may carry MOD, POSS and APPOS. Example, "sweet mead": `[{"lex_id": "<mead>", "role": "NP"}, {"lex_id": "<sweet>", "role": "MOD", "head": 0}]` = swéh₂du médʰu. No V, SUBJ, OBJ, no `neg`.
- **Apposition** (role `APPOS` with `head`): a second noun phrase naming the same thing, in its head's case and number: "Dyēus, the father", "the horses, immortal ones". A noun keeps its own gender; an adjective takes the head's (it means "the immortal ones", a separate phrase, where a MOD means "immortal"). It follows its head (`"before": true` puts it first: "father Dyēus") and may have its own MOD/POSS. Use it for epithet chains on gods and heroes (Vedic, Avestan and Greek hymns put honorific epithets in apposition to the name), and wherever a daughter text has the adjective after the noun as a separate phrase.

## What makes a good text
1. **Continuity.** Keep characters and topics across clauses; refer back with pronouns (`closed:anaphoric_i` or `closed:demonstrative_so` with the right `gender`, or pro-drop: the verb's person and number alone) rather than repeating nouns every clause. Open new sentences with `particle: "nu"` ("then") to move a story forward; join closely linked clauses with `conj: "kʷe"`.
2. **Variety of syntax.** Mix main clauses with relative clauses (`closed:relative_yo` + correlative `closed:demonstrative_so`), participial phrases, questions and answers, commands (IMP), wishes (OPT: "may X …"), exhortations (SBJV 1st plural: "let us …"), negation and prohibition (neg + IMP = "do not!"). About 20% of clauses verb-initial (VSO) for emphasis or in narrative sequence.
3. **Genre voice.** Hymn: invocation (VOC), praise of the god's deeds (AOR, PST), and a closing request (IMP or OPT). Make hymns *poetry*, not lists (review of t002: "flat, repetitive prose"): praise through relative clauses ("you who freed the waters, who split the stone"), epithet chains on the god (adjectives and genitives: "shining daughter of Sky"), deliberate anaphora (the same word opening three lines, then a turn), a myth told in two or three aorist clauses, and a request that answers the praise. Keep one person of address: once the god is "you" (VOC + 2nd-person verbs), don't switch to "he" in the same hymn. Prayer/charm: imperatives and optatives, repetition. Law: "whoever does X, let him pay Y" (relative clause + SBJV/OPT/IMP), lists of cases. Myth/epic: narrative past (AOR for single events, PST for background), participles, speeches. Dialogue: turns with questions, answers that repeat the question's verb, vocatives. Riddle: describe the thing by indirect, surprising features (what it does, what it is like, a paradox) over several clauses before the answer; don't name or nearly name it, and vary the form (not every riddle "Who is it that …? It is …") (review of t007: riddles too easy and uniform).
4. **Inherited phrasing.** Your slice has a `formulas` list: inherited phrases reconstructed from matching daughter-language wording (each has its lexemes and a note on how to build it, e.g. ḱléwos + MOD ń̥dʰgʷʰitos = "imperishable fame"). Use at least one formula in every text where it fits naturally (hymns, epic, praise: almost always). Don't force them into laws or dialogues where they would be odd.
5. **Meter (hymns, prayers, charms, praise, laments, wedding and work songs).** Old Indo-European verse is counted in syllables: lines of 8 (Vedic gāyatrī) or 11 (triṣṭubh). **Every verse line must have exactly 8 or 11 syllables** (12, jagatī, only if the plan record declares `"meter": 12` next to `"genre"`). A verse line is a sentence: the clauses from one `"break": true` to the next. The metered genres are listed in `pie.meter.METERED_GENRES`; syllables are counted by `pie/meter.py` (a laryngeal between consonants or after a consonant at the end of a word is a syllable: ph₂tḗr 2, méǵh₂ 2, h₃ésth₁ 2; an initial h₂stḗr is 1; r̥ l̥ m̥ n̥ i u are syllables; word order never changes a count).
   - **The gate is exact:** `meter_exact>=0.90`, the share of verse lines with exactly 8 or 11 syllables, over the metered texts of the batch. A batch below 0.90 FAILs. After the initial run, `corpus/tier3/<batch>-initial.jsonl` is deleted, but `data/generation/batches/<batch>-initial.json` (and later `<batch>.json`) reports `meter_exact` (gated), `meter_near` (within ±1, reported only; `meter_fit` is the same number under its old name) and `meter_cadence` (whether exact lines end in the Vedic close, information only).
   - **Use the meter tool before the final run:** `uv run python -I scripts/meter_report.py data/generation/plans/<batch>.jsonl --misses` lists every verse line that is off target with its count, target, gap, plan id and clause indices, and the measured edits that reach the target: add or drop the particle nu or -ge, join or unjoin a clause with -kʷe, drop or add an epithet (a slice adjective as MOD) or a vocative, change a noun's number (dual or plural), split a long sentence at a clause or merge two short ones. It writes nothing. Prefer edits that keep the meaning (particles, -kʷe, split/merge); when an edit adds a word or changes number, update the English to match. Re-run it until every line is on target, then run the final `realize_plans.py`.
   - Improve meter in the repair pass by adding or dropping a word with these edits, never by changing the meaning.

## Rules (all still apply)
6. Use `lex_id`s from your slice (`lexemes`, `closed`, `adverbs`), or ones you find with the lexicon query tool:
   `uv run python -I scripts/lexq.py <english word> …` (`--id <lex_id>` shows a word's cells; Tier 3b adds
   `--tier3b`). The slice is the batch's suggested vocabulary; lexq answers "what is the word for X?" over every
   word a planner may use, with the same fields and flags. Never invent ids. (2026-10-30: the slice alone was
   too small, and planners wrote around words the lexicon has.)
7. Exactly one V slot per verbal clause (nominal and np clauses have none); usually 2–7 slots. **Impersonal verbs** (weather, time of day: "it rains", "it is getting dark", "it dawns") take **no subject at all**: the clause is just the V slot (plus any adverbs). Never add a dummy demonstrative "it", which the PIE would read as "that" (Ved. várṣati 'it rains'; review of t010).
8. Verb features must be in that verb's `features` list. A single past event in narrative is AOR (or PST only if the verb has no aorist); a perfect is never a narrative past ("is born", "knows" are states). Proverbs and general truths ("a guest is holy", "fire burns") use the present; a verb that has only aorist forms takes the gnomic aorist (AOR, IND), never a subjunctive. `deponent: true` verbs take voice "MID" only. Aspect: PRS/PST = ongoing or habitual, AOR = a completed single event, PRF = a present state ("knows", "is afraid", "has seen"). For a single past event use AOR when the slice has an aorist of the root, otherwise PST.
9. **Pro-drop:** no 1st/2nd-person pronoun subject just to mark person; put `person`/`num` on the verb. **A clause without a SUBJ defaults to 3rd singular**: whenever its implied subject is anything else ("…and *they* drove her away", "…and *you* arrived", two people), set `person` and `num` on its verb. The engine does not carry a subject over from the previous clause (English "and" changes subjects freely). Review of t004: a plural subject left implicit came out singular. Pronoun subjects only for contrast or emphasis.
10. Pronoun slots used on their own must give a `gender` matching their referent. "What" = `closed:interrogative_kwi`, gender "n".
11. Prohibitions are `neg: true` + mood IMP, with verbs that have PRS or AOR forms (never PRF). `neg: true` + 1st-person SBJV is "let me/us not"; for "I shall not …" write `neg: "ne"`. An IMP beside a VOC needs `person` (2 = command to the addressee). The perfect has no 2du/3du: a dual subject or addressee takes a present or aorist.
12. **Voice:** a transitive verb whose English subject undergoes the action ("the wheel broke", "dress yourself") takes "MID". Verbs tagged `valency: "tr"` need an OBJ in the active.
13. **Questions and answers:** an answer repeats the question's verb lexeme unless the English changes the verb.
14. **The English says what the plan says**, no more and no less; every content word needs a slot. Write the English as good, plain prose (it is the translation readers will see). **Use each lexeme only in a sense its gloss gives**: never write "chariot" for a lexeme glossed "wheel", or "cave" for "something hollow". If the slice lacks the word, say what the lexeme means ("into a hollow"), or restructure. A blind back-translator checks every text against its English (review of t001).
17. **Number:** natural pairs take the dual (`num: "DU"`): a chariot's two horses, two eyes, hands, the two parents, two brothers. Only the nominative, accusative and vocative dual are reconstructible; in other cases (genitive, dative, instrumental, …) the engine uses the plural form for a dual slot, which is expected. A neuter plural subject takes a singular verb automatically (an inherited rule).
18. **Adjectives as names** ("the Swift One", "the Bright One") need `gender` on their slot: "f" for a daughter or goddess. Two coordinated subjects ("the father and the son") take a dual verb (`num: "DU"` on the verb); with a 1st or 2nd person among them, the verb takes that person: "I and my brother" = `person: 1, num: "DU"` (review of t005).
18b. **Gender of address:** agent nouns in -tōr/-tḗr are masculine; don't address a goddess with one ("giver" to Dawn): use her name, an adjective, or a feminine noun.
19. **Pronouns:** in long texts, re-name a character with a noun when two characters of the same gender are in play, so "he"/"him" stays unambiguous; use `pie-lex-swos` ("his/her own") for reflexive possession.
15. **Word choice:** prefer the lexeme with the higher `attested_in`. `gloss_approximate` lexemes only when the meaning fits plainly and no plainly glossed synonym is in the slice. `gloss_source: "nil"` glosses come from a dictionary of nominal formations and are reliable.
16. **Diversity (gated):** any one lexeme at most max(6, 0.5% of the batch's tokens) times; `theme_core` lexemes (the god or thing the batch is about) up to max(15, 1.5% of tokens). The copula is exempt.
