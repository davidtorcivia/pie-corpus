## Phase 1 notes

- **The source is clean.** Only 4 of 1,931 headwords are outside the inventory. Normalization fired 14 times (3 homoglyphs, 6 laryngeal rings, 5 dot-below syllabics). The `\w` escape occurs in 17 forms.
- **Wiktionary's declension module corrupts some tables.** Where a stem template has parentheses or Ø, the dual `-h₁` becomes `é₁`/`Ø₁`. Those 90 tables are excluded at the source.
- **Wiktionary etymon ids can't disambiguate homographs.** kaikki copies them across sibling sections, so 8 homograph sets collided. Root ids therefore carry a gloss tag on every id (`pie-root-men-think`, `pie-root-men-stay`) and persist in a committed registry. Rebuilds are idempotent.
- **Provisional review** (subagent, knowledge-only, 50 entries): 20 ok, 26 questionable, 4 wrong. The triage is in `docs/audits/phase1-lexicon-provisional-review.md`. It found **one real builder bug**: same-form derivatives under different homograph roots were merged, e.g. two *kretós*. Fixing it split 140 homonyms. Duplicate reflexes and unflagged loans are fixed as well. The other 3 "wrong" links are Wiktionary's own placements, logged as disputed. The id registry was reset once for this fix; safe because nothing references ids yet.
- **Gaps:** 344 words/derivatives with no root link (mostly pronouns, particles, opaque nouns); 58 etymon parts that point at ambiguous homographs.

## IDEAS.md disposition

Everything in IDEAS.md is folded into PLAN.md, and nothing is rejected outright. These parts were **modified**:

- **Sound-law checker (2).** (a) The Germanic target is **Proto-Germanic**, not Gothic. Wiktionary descendants include PGmc reconstructions, and Gothic needs a further layer of rules; Gothic can be added later. (b) **Inflected forms are checked only against a curated list of regular pairs.** The IDEAS example itself shows why: Gk φέρει is not the regular outcome of *bʰéreti* (the regular one would be *φέρετι), and Lat. *fert* continues an athematic form. A blanket inflected-form check would mostly measure analogy, not engine errors. (c) **Brugmann's law** goes behind a flag with separate counts, because it is contested.
- **Task-format eval (4).** Cognate→PIE eval holds out **whole root families**, otherwise derivatives in training leak the answer. Paradigm exact-match is scored against the golden scholarly paradigms (Phase 2), not only engine output, so the benchmark doesn't just reward imitating our own engine.
- **Modern PIE (5).** Added as Tier 3b, as proposed, with one extra guard: neologisms are **never** loaded into the validator lexicon for Tiers 1–3, so a coinage can't leak into the "plausible archaic" tiers as if it were a real root.
- **Meter (6).** Accepted. It needs one deterministic syllabifier with documented choices (laryngeal vocalization, Sievers' law as a flag), because syllable counts in PIE depend on those choices. Conventions (2026-10-28 audit, `pie/meter.py` docstring, `docs/audits/meter-census.md`): a laryngeal is a syllable between consonants **and after a consonant at word end** (méǵh₂ = Ved. máhi, 2; this case used to count 0), not word-initially (#HC, #HHC), not after another laryngeal, and not after a diphthong in -y (thematic optative -oyh₁t = Ved. -et; dwóyh₁ = Ved. dvé); r̥ l̥ m̥ n̥ i u are nuclei as the engine marks them; no Sievers or Lindeman by default. Tier 3 gate `meter_exact>=0.90`: verse lines (sentences) of metered genres with exactly 8 or 11 syllables (12 if declared); the old ±1 share is reported as `meter_near`/`meter_fit`.
- **Dashboard (7).** The metrics are in STATUS.md now. The HTML Artifact version is deferred until there is data to show (Phase 1+).

## Known limitations

Three limitations of this release, recorded separately (accent policy, David 2026-10-24; hold-out swap, 2026-10-28).

(1) **Missing accent information (accent: unresolved).** 621 usable Wiktionary lexemes have a headword without an accent and no sourced spec supplies one, so their paradigms are accentless (`unresolved_lexical_accent` in data/morph/accent_unresolved.json, by family: thematic nouns, thematic neuters, thematic presents, and a few others). This is missing information, not evidence that the words were unaccented. They stay usable: the morphological `usable` flag (inflection capability) and the planner-selection gate (scripts/make_slice.py, which requires an accented headword) are unchanged. That gate also excludes 11 paradigms whose headword is unaccented although the spec supplies the accent: a later item, not changed here. How the status is carried: `Lexicon.accent_status(lex_id)` → "resolved" | "unresolved"; the validator's accent lint lists such tokens under `accent_unresolved` (unresolved accent, missing information), apart from `accentless` (an accent defect) and `accent_representation`; scripts/realize_plans.py's batch summary counts their tokens, lexemes and records (`accent_unresolved`); pie/export.py marks every task whose target or a token comes from such a lexeme with `"accent": "unresolved"` (translation and gloss tasks of Tier 2/3 records, inflection and cognate-to-PIE tasks). The accent-sensitive evaluation view export/eval_tasks_accent_specified.jsonl omits those tasks; they stay in the full eval and in training, identifiable by the flag, and any score presented as testing fully specified accent must use that view (export/README.md). In the default export before this change: 7 such eval tasks (3 inflection, e.g. *kreydʰrom* with an unaccented target; 4 cognate-to-PIE); in the stored non-pilot Tier 3 corpus 11 unresolved lexemes (48 tokens, 40 records; with the pilots 16 / 57 / 49; none in Tier 2). Those 11 were searched for a sourced accent (Fortson, Ringe, Beekes, M&A, LIV², EDG, EWAia, de Vaan, Kroonen, Derksen, Matasović, NIL, Wiktionary's other pages): none is decided by the evidence, so none got an override, and each has a one-line reason in the manifest (`reasons`). Two came closest: *ḱerh₂wos* (Gk κεραός is oxytone, but it is the adjective 'horned', and the substantive may retract, Fortson §6.72) and *teh₂yeti* (only a Wiktionary synonym list prints *téh₂yeti*). *h₂eysos* probably has the wrong formation (de Vaan: s-stem neuter *h₂eis-os-), a separate item. A sourced accent goes into data/lexicon/overrides.jsonl as a checked spec with its citation, and the id leaves the manifest.

(2) **Notation and prosodic model (representation exceptions).** Spellings such as the vocative *ph₂ter* are kept for this release. The project's isolated-address (initial) vocative accent (Fortson §6.52) is kept for ordinary vocatives whose initial prominence can be represented; where the first syllable's nucleus is laryngeal-containing in the segmental spelling (*ph₂tér* → voc. *ph₂ter*, beside Ved. pítar, Gk πάτερ; *dʰh₁tó-* beside Ved. híta), `pie.morph._initial_accent` leaves the form without a visible accent rather than move it to a later vowel (no *ph₂tér* as a vocative). This is how the initial-accent convention interacts with the segmental representation, not a claim that the accent historically fell on a laryngeal. The notation has no way to mark an accent on a syllabic laryngeal (Hackstein prints *ph₂́ter* for the Sky-Father vocative; supporting it needs coordinated changes in transliteration, parsing, rendering, accent removal, indexing and validation: deferred). Inventory: 30 lexemes, 111 primary cells (not 30 forms), frozen in the manifest as exact (lex_id, gender, cell, primary form) combinations (`representation_exceptions`), so a new, unrelated accent loss cannot hide among them; the lint lists them under `accent_representation`, exported tasks carry `"accent": "representation"`. The engine also has no phrase-level prosody: phrase- or clause-initial vocatives get initial prominence, phrase-internal ones do not (verse-line starts often coincide with such boundaries, but "first word of a printed line" is not the rule), and this positional pattern is not modelled. Its analysis is unsettled: Gunkel distinguishes phrase-initial from phrase-internal vocatives, argues that Ved. pítar ~ Gk πάτερ does not force an accented schwa or an accent-bearing laryngeal in PIE and proposes lexically unaccented vocatives; Keydana ("Accent or intonation? The vocative in Vedic") treats the initial high and non-initial low tone as intonation, with the vocative's word-level accent the same as the nominative's. The positional surface pattern is firmer than either analysis (data/research/literature: gunkel-vocative, keydana-vocative, hackstein-skyfather, metadata to verify).

(3) **Benchmark hold-out swapped (2026-10-28, David: "Yes swap out the held word families"). Scores before and after the swap are not comparable.** Until this date `pie.export.eval_root` held out root families by a 10% sha256 hash of root_id, which also held out core formula vocabulary: 19 of the 138 hashed families, among them *dʰugh₂tḗr*'s (`pie-root-dhewgh-hit`), so "Daughter of Sky" could not reach planners. The hold-out is now a frozen list, data/export/held_roots.json, written by scripts/build_holdout.py (selection documented in its docstring) and read through the same `eval_root`, which scripts/make_slice.py and pie/export.py both call. data/export/protected_roots.json lists 212 families that are never held out, each with its reasons, derived from the data: the formula menu, the words of data/formulas/formulas.jsonl (mapped as build_formula_menu maps them), Tier 2 secure lexemes, make_slice's CORE_NOUNS / CONVERSATION / CORE_VERBS, numerals (pos num) and Swadesh-type kin, body-part and natural-world nouns (the one hand-written list is the concept words; the lexemes are found from their glosses). The new set is 147 families (round(0.10 × 1469)), drawn only from families that no Tier 2 training line, Tier 3/3b record (admitted or not), plan or planner slice uses, and stratified (task-bearing × branch count × verbal/nominal root) to the old hash draw's mix, hash-ranked within each stratum. All 147 are unseen in training (old: 102 of 138 effective, 34 bearing eval tasks; new: 75), so the cognate→PIE and inflection eval grew from 48 + 48 to 96 + 93 tasks on the same corpus. The new families are less attested than the old hash draw (mean 5.5 against 7.1 reflex languages; 2.8 against 3.2 branches) but more than the families the old benchmark actually evaluated (3.7, 1.8), and their tasks lean to verbs (verb 105, noun 31 tasks against the old draw's 203 / 185): the noun-rich families are nearly all in training or protected. Re-running build_holdout.py selects a different set as the corpus grows; it is frozen on purpose. A family added to the lexicon later is not held out. tests/test_export.py re-derives the cheap protected sources from the live data and fails if one of them reaches a held family (Tier 2 is still being regenerated: re-check after it lands).

**Schema clarification (2026-10-25, DOC-01).** Raw corpus records/tokens do not store the derived task-level accent flags. Raw consumers join token `lex_id` values to `data/morph/accent_unresolved.json` (through `Lexicon.accent_status` / `accent_status`); exported tasks carry the derived status. For engine-built Tier 2/3 record tasks, any unresolved token yields `accent: unresolved`; otherwise a matching frozen representation cell/form yields `accent: representation`. Inflection targets use the frozen lexeme/gender/cell/primary-form tuple; cognate-to-PIE tasks derive unresolved status from the target lexeme. Tier 1 authorial accents are not flagged this way. Representation limitations and genuinely unaccented clitics remain separate from lexical uncertainty. Unresolved lexemes remain morphologically usable under the existing policy. The evaluation invariant is `eval_tasks_accent_specified == [task for task in eval_tasks if task.get("accent") != "unresolved"]`; representation tasks remain in that view. This clarification changes no linguistic decisions, stored corpus schema, or export behavior.
